# E10 — Battle: interception, salvos, damage and repair

**Brief:** `.ai/specs/product-brief.md` — R19, R26, R30, R51–R57, R65, R67, N07, D16, plus the new R69–R80 and D17 added with this spec.
**Revised 2026-11-17** after the creator's corrections, before any of it shipped: one interceptor per player per arrival, an intercepted ship held only while its interceptor fights (R70, R74), and a battle when a ship stays in Space next to others (R80).
**Source:** the creator's rules and answers of 2026-11-17 (this session), and the creator's vault note `10-projects/Trader/Battle.md` (the battle table and damage rules), with the two table corrections the creator gave.
**Siblings:** E04 Ship-jumps (the phase battle lives in), E07 Hulls and modules (the stats a battle uses), E09 Shipyards (refit, where repair now lives).
**Prototype:** none. The new panels follow the design system (`docs/design-system.md`).

## 📝 TLDR

Ships in **Space** can now fight.

1. **Interception.** When a ship arrives in a system's Space, every other player with ships already in Space there may pick one of them to try to stop it. Each try is an opposed roll: intercept + d6 + crew (+1 A, −1 D) + Battle comm (+1). The higher total wins, and a tie goes to the arriving ship. A ship that tries gives up its own jump this turn.
2. **Battle.** If the arriving ship is caught, or a ship lands in Space where other players' ships are, the jumps stop and a battle runs at that system as a sub-phase of Ship-jumps. Every ship in Space there takes part, whoever intercepted. Each round, every player secretly orders each of their ships to **fire** at one enemy ship (with or without rockets), or to **break away**, which an intercepted ship can't do while a ship that caught it still fights. All salvos land together. The defender places the hits on its hull and modules. The battle lasts while two or more players still have ships in it.
3. **After the battle**, every surviving ship that was in it chooses Port, Planet or Space, and the jumps go on.
4. **Damage stays** until it's repaired during Investment at a shipyard system.

Ships can now also "jump" within their own system (from Port or Planet into Space, for example), so a ship can move up to meet an enemy.

## 📝 Rules

| Id | Rule |
|---|---|
| R69 | **Moving within a system.** A ship may jump to the system it is already at. It arrives in that system's Space like any other jump, can be intercepted (R70), and then chooses where it ends up (R30). It counts as the ship's jump for the turn. It has no route, so no jump chance (R68, shipped meanwhile in `2026-11-17-jump-chance.md`) is rolled: it always succeeds. A damaged Safe jump module adds nothing to a jump's chance (R76). |
| R70 | **Interception.** When a ship arrives in Space (any jump, R69 included), every *other* player who has ships in Space at that system (arrived earlier this turn, moved up from Port or Planet, or there since an earlier turn) is asked, all at once, which one of those ships tries to intercept it, if any: at most one per player. All of them still fight if a battle starts. Each try is an opposed roll: interceptor *intercept + d6 + crew modifier + Battle comm modifier* against the arriving ship's same total. The interceptor must be strictly higher; a tie goes to the arriving ship. The ship is intercepted if any try succeeds. Every roll is public in the log. |
| R71 | **Modifiers.** Crew class A +1, B and C 0, D −1. A working (undamaged) Battle comm module +1 (one per ship, however many are fitted). They change interception only, not firing. Battle comm is military (R56), so no player can have one yet. |
| R72 | **An interceptor gives up its jump.** A ship that tried to intercept, whether or not it succeeded, can't jump later this turn. It can still choose Port, Planet or Space after a battle (R77). |
| R73 | **Battle.** A successful interception stops the Ship-jump phase: nobody jumps until the battle at that system is over. Every ship in Space at that system is in the battle, whoever owns it. The arriving ship is *intercepted* by each ship whose try succeeded; the others aren't intercepted. |
| R74 | **A round.** Every player with ships in the battle gives each of them one secret order: **fire** at one ship of another player in the battle, with or without **rockets**; or **break away**, except for an intercepted ship while a ship that intercepted it is still fighting: once its interceptors are destroyed or have broken away, it may break away too. When every player has given their orders, they are revealed and all of them resolve together, so a ship destroyed this round still fires. A salvo at a ship that breaks away this round misses. A ship that breaks away leaves the battle, still in Space, and can't be fired at again in it. |
| R75 | **A salvo.** Attack = the firer's weapons (hull + working modules, R53) + 2d6. With rockets, each working module that has a rocket value (the figure in brackets) counts that instead; the hull and modules without one count as usual. Rockets are unlimited (canonical). The battle table gives the hits from the attack and the target's shields (1–4): **2–4:** 0 / 0 / 0 / 0; **5–6:** 1 / 0 / 0 / 0; **7–9:** 1 / 1 / 0 / 0; **10–12:** 2 / 1 / 1 / 0; **13–16:** 2 / 2 / 1 / 1; **17–20:** 3 / 2 / 2 / 1; **21–25:** 4 / 3 / 2 / 2; **26–30:** 5 / 4 / 3 / 2; **31+:** 6 / 5 / 4 / 3 (columns are shields 1 / 2 / 3 / 4; the 21–25 and 26–30 rows are the creator's corrections of the vault note). After a rocket salvo with at least one hit, a d6 is rolled: equal to or below the hits, the target is destroyed at once. |
| R76 | **Damage.** The target's owner places every hit a ship took in the round. The hull takes hits up to its shields, and one more destroys the ship. A working module takes 1 hit and becomes damaged, or 2 hits and is destroyed. A damaged module takes 1 more hit and is destroyed. A damaged module counts as not there (no weapons, rockets, cargo, passengers or Battle comm) but still needs its crew and can be repaired. A destroyed module is gone. A ship whose hull and modules can't absorb all its hits is destroyed. When damage leaves less cargo space than the POLY and ISO aboard, the owner chooses which units are lost. Hull damage does nothing else (the vault note). |
| R77 | **The end of a battle.** After each round, destroyed ships are removed with everything aboard, and a destroyed ship's loss is public in the log. The battle ends when fewer than two players have ships left in it. Then every surviving ship that was in the battle, including those that broke away, is placed by its owner at Port, Planet or Space of that system, all players at once. The jumps then go on from where they stopped. |
| R78 | **What a battle shows.** The hull, modules, damage and crew class of every ship in a battle are shown to every player while it lasts (an exception to R57). Orders stay secret until the round resolves. Every roll, salvo, hit count and destruction is public in the log. Where a player placed their hits is shown as the ship's damage afterwards; the cargo lost isn't logged (R29). |
| R79 | **Repair.** During Investment, a damaged ship in a shipyard (in the yard, or in Port at Beta Hydri, Epsilon Eridani or Tau Ceti, as R65) can be repaired: all its hull damage for the hull's repair price (the table's "repair" column, flat), plus 10 HT for each damaged module (4 HT for a Cargo module). It's one action that repairs everything, refused when the player can't pay. It's private (R57) and not logged. Damage is also shown on the Fleet tab. |
| R80 | **Staying in Space.** When a ship that jumped lands in Space (R30) where other players' ships are in Space, a battle starts there as R73, with nobody intercepted, so every ship may break away, and a salvo at a ship breaking away misses (R74): declining costs nothing. Placing ships after a battle (R77) never starts another. |

## 📋 Stories and acceptance criteria

### E10-S01 — A ship can move within its own system

1. [R69] `POST /ship-jumps/jumps` with the ship's own system is accepted; the ship arrives in Space there and the jump is logged as usual ("Wayfarer → Tau Ceti").
2. [R69] The jump destination list on the Ship-jumps screen includes the ship's own system.

### E10-S02 — Ships in Space can intercept an arriving ship

1. [R70] After a jump, when other players have ships in Space at the destination, the turn waits for an interception decision from each of them. Without such ships the ship lands as before.
2. [R70] `POST /ship-jumps/interception` with `{ ship }` (null or absent means none) records a player's choice; refused (`409`) when the player isn't asked or has answered already, and (`400`) for a ship that isn't theirs in Space there.
3. [R70, R71] When everyone asked has answered, every try is rolled and logged: "Interception: Lantern (2 + d6 4 + 0 = 6) vs Wayfarer (2 + d6 3 + 0 = 5): intercepted". Ties fail.
4. [R72] Every ship that tried can't jump later this turn.
5. [R70] Nobody intercepts → the arriving ship's owner chooses where it lands, as before; staying in Space next to other players' ships starts a battle (R80).

### E10-S03 — A battle runs in rounds of secret, simultaneous orders

1. [R73] An interception starts a battle at that system with every ship in Space there; the log says "Battle at Tau Ceti: Wayfarer, Lantern, Kestrel". Jumping, landing and finishing a jump slot are refused (`409`) while it lasts.
2. [R74] `POST /ship-jumps/battle/orders` with `{ orders: [{ ship, action: FIRE | BREAK_AWAY, target, rockets }] }` gives one order for each of the player's ships in the battle. Refused with `400` or `409` for: a missing or extra ship; a target that isn't another player's ship in the battle; break away for a ship whose interceptor still fights; orders already given this round.
3. [R74] Orders are visible only to their player until everyone has given theirs; the game shows who has.
4. [R74, R75] Then every salvo resolves together and is logged: "Salvo: Lantern → Wayfarer, 6 + 2d6 9 = 15 vs shields 2: 2 hits". A rocket salvo also logs the d6 and, if it kills, "Wayfarer is destroyed". A salvo at a ship that broke away logs "misses: Kestrel broke away".
5. [R76] Each player whose ships took hits that round (and weren't destroyed outright) places them: `POST /ship-jumps/battle/hits` with `{ ships: [{ ship, hull, modules: [{ module, damaged, hits }], jettison: { POLY, ISO } }] }`. Refused unless the hits add up exactly, each module entry is one fitted module in that condition (`damaged`), a working module takes 1–2 hits and a damaged one 1, the hull takes at most shields + 1 minus its damage, and the jettison is exactly the cargo that no longer fits. A ship that can't absorb its hits is destroyed without asking.
6. [R77] When everyone has placed, destroyed ships are removed (logged "Wayfarer is destroyed") and the next round starts, or the battle ends.

### E10-S04 — Surviving ships land, and the jumps go on

1. [R77] `POST /ship-jumps/battle/landing` with `{ ships: [{ ship, location }] }` places each surviving ship of that player from the battle at Port, Planet or Space; logged as landings are now.
2. [R77] When every such player has landed, the battle is gone; the jump slot goes on, or ends if the jumper has nothing left to jump (S02-6).
3. [R77] A player who lost every ship in the battle isn't asked.

### E10-S05 — Damage shows and can be repaired

1. [R76] A damaged module adds nothing to weapons, rockets, cargo or passengers; the Fleet tab shows hull damage ("Hull 1 / 2") and which modules are damaged.
2. [R79] `POST /investment/ships/{ship}/repair` repairs everything on a ship in a shipyard during Investment, after Collect, and charges the price; refused with `400` when it isn't damaged, isn't at a shipyard, or the player can't pay.
3. [R78] While a battle lasts, its ships' hulls, modules, damage and crew class are in the game response for every player.

## 🔧 Design

- **Domain.** `Ship` gains `hullDamage: Int` and `damagedModules: List<Module>` (a multiset; a subset of `modules`). Derived stats use working modules. `Ship.takeHits(placement)`, `Ship.isDestroyedBy(...)`, `Ship.repaired()`, `Ship.interceptScore(d6)`. `BattleTable` (a value class over the attack total) gives hits by shields. `Dice` gains `d6()`.
- **Turn** gains `interception: Interception?` and `battle: Battle?`. `Interception` holds the system, the arriving ship and its owner, the players asked and their answers. `Battle` holds the system, round, stage (`ORDERS`, `HITS`, `LANDING`), the ships still in it, the intercepted ship, the ships that left it, this round's orders, the hits waiting to be placed, and who has acted. `Game` coordinates (player ships, the log). `Battle` owns the round's rules.
- **Persistence.** `TurnItem` maps the two new fields through `InterceptionItem` and `BattleItem`. `ShipItem` maps the damage fields, defaulted to none for older ships (BACKWARD_COMPATIBILITY.md §7).
- **Web.** Four new Ship-jumps endpoints and one Investment endpoint. `turn.interception` and `turn.battle` in the game response (public parts), and `you.battle` (my orders, my hits to place). The Ship-jumps screen gets an Interception panel and a Battle panel, and Fleet shows damage and a Repair button.

## 💡 Possible improvements (not built)

- **Stances (the creator liked it; parked for later).** Each round every ship secretly picks **Fire**, **Evade** (hold fire; each attack on it this round has its 2d6 total cut by its intercept value) or **Break away** for anyone (an opposed intercept roll against the best enemy; win and it leaves after this round's hits). That brings agility into firing and gives intercepted ships another way out than destroying their interceptor.
- Police in Space (R31) joining or stopping fights, and legal consequences for firing in high-legal systems.
- Military parts through opportunities, which makes Battle comm and the rocket-heavy modules reachable.

## Out of scope

- Fights in Port or on a Planet; interception on departure.
- Anything AI-controlled; any reward for destroying a ship.
