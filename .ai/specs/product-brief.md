## Decision summary

The creator is building their own idea — an online version of a 1980s trading/space board game — and already has working domain logic (star systems, goods, dice-resolved trade offers) and static UI mockups, but no multi-game, multiplayer, or persistence layer yet. The decision this session supports is the shape of that missing layer: how many concurrent games are hosted, how shared vs. confidential state is kept and synced, how players authenticate, and how simultaneous-but-timed phases resolve.

The creator chose: short-interval polling for state sync (D01), a document-store persistence layer (D02), passwordless email-code login (D03), and a timer-based auto-skip for silent players in simultaneous phases (D04). They also revealed that confidential advantages are acquired and tradeable between players during play, not dealt out once (R06), which pushes the data model toward an event/ledger shape rather than a static per-player profile.

The most consequential untested beliefs are whether confidential reads can be scoped to the right player before any endpoint ships (A05) and whether a document store's default consistency model will reliably deliver the "a few seconds" cross-player visibility the creator wants (A01) — see Riskiest assumptions, which also now covers a timer/write race in D04 (A04) and the persistence schema shape (A03), both raised by an independent skeptic pass over this draft. The next action is for the creator (or whoever architects the skeleton) to settle the session→player→game authorization check (A05) and pick a concrete store and consistency setting against the latency target (A01) before building the polling+persistence path.

A 2026-10-20 quick follow-up resolved Q02 (what happens when a player disconnects mid-game): resume is indefinite via re-login (D05), and once a player has already been auto-skipped for one phase, later phases pause for them rather than continuing to auto-skip (D06); no kick or replace mechanism exists for v1 (N04). Together these mean one long-absent player can stall a game forever for the rest of the group, with no escape hatch — the creator accepted this as a known risk rather than closing it (A06), since this lands in Scope/Later, not the Now skeleton.

A second 2026-10-20 follow-up, scoped only to trading rules at the creator's request, turned the thin R03 ("dice-resolved trades") into a complete resolution model pulled from the existing code and confirmed or corrected by the creator, then refined across several further corrections once the model was checked against the code and against itself. The settled model: a trade resolves by dice + indicator (R08); winning grants a right to trade between 1 and a formula-computed maximum, not a forced amount (R13, superseding R09's original "not chosen by the player" framing); the system's reference price then moves granularly, by the amount actually executed, while the executed trade itself always settles at the winner's own stated price regardless of how far the reference price moves (R14); a bid/ask has no price ceiling for winning the auction, but an extreme price's effect on both the amount ceiling and the reference-price move saturates at the table's edges — at most +7 (buy) or -6 (sell) — rather than being unbounded or crashing the lookup (R15); a won trade also updates the winner's money and market-position tier (R10, R11 — Wholesaler's guaranteed extra 6 units, Dictator's trump power); a sell is capped by ship/warehouse holdings (R10) while a buy is capped only by what the player can afford (R12), never by storage — buying more than fits in storage is legal, the excess is simply lost (R16). Reputation and loans, though already present as unused fields on `Player`, are explicitly out of the MVP trading loop (N05). The one real gap this surfaced: the ship/warehouse inventory model that R10's sell-cap depends on has only a mockup sketch (`ships.html`/`warehouses.html` show per-ship cargo meters and per-depot capacity figures) that was never confirmed as the actual rule — capacities, how goods move between ship and warehouse, starting stock are all still open (Q03). That's a distinct domain from trading and gets its own narrow session, the same way this one was kept to trading only.

A third 2026-10-20 follow-up, scoped to turn-taking, closed the biggest structural gap: nothing in the code tracks a current turn or decides when it advances — `TradeOffer.turn` is just a client-supplied tag. The creator confirmed a v1 turn is exactly three phases, repeated: Bidding, Ship-jumps, and Trading, in that order — the other 5 of the original 8 board-game phases don't exist for v1 at all (N06, corrected from an initial "2 phases" after an independent review caught that R04/Key-flow step 4 still treated ship jumps as live; the phase order itself was later corrected again, see below). Bidding is simultaneous and secret: players submit any number of offers across systems/goods, invisible to everyone else; signaling done is a readiness flag, not a commit, so a player may keep adding offers until Bidding actually closes, once all have signaled done or D04's timer catches a straggler (R17) — this exposed a real, concrete implementation gap: today's `/api/trade-offers` endpoint already lists every player's offers publicly, which directly contradicts R17 and ties into the architecture brief's A05 (confidential-read authorization). Trading is sequential and public: every system+good with pending offers resolves one at a time, in an order set entirely up front by whichever player holds that turn's initiative — a mechanism decided during Bidding, distinct from the existing per-offer submission-sequence tiebreak — with every resolution visible to all players as it happens (R18).

A fourth 2026-10-20 follow-up, combining initiative, ship jumps, and money at the creator's request, closed the remaining mechanism gaps and reframed the MVP's actual purpose: this build exists to playtest login, turns, trading, and visuals, not to be feature-complete. Ship jumps are instant for v1 — no fuel, hull damage, repair, or multi-cycle transit, despite the ships mockup showing all of that (R19, N07). That initiative (Q05 from the prior session) is itself an all-pay money auction run during Bidding: every player who wants to compete bids money, 0 or more, and pays their own bid regardless of outcome; the winner is the highest bid plus a d2 roll, ties rerolled (R21) — a real money sink distinct from trading. An independent review then asked whether the Ship-jump phase's initiative-set order actually does anything, which surfaced the real reason ships exist at all: a ship is how a player stores and moves goods between systems, and trading at a system needs a ship physically there (R23) — so the creator corrected two things in the same breath: the phase order is Bidding, then Ship-jumps, then Trading (not Trading before Ship-jumps as first drafted), and a destination is chosen openly during the Ship-jump phase itself, one player at a time in initiative order — not secretly during Bidding as first drafted (R20). Goods bought at a system with no ship present are simply lost, the same fate as R16's storage overflow (R23). Each player starts with a fixed money balance and exactly 3 ships of predetermined cargo capacity; no purchasing or commissioning exists for v1 (R22) — the actual starting numbers are still open (Q06), as is whether declining to bid for initiative differs from bidding exactly 0 (Q07).

A 2026-10-28 follow-up came from the creator walking a clickable prototype of the Deck (map), system details and Trading screens, and changed several rules. Money is now private to each player, a house rule departing from the original board game; the engine enforces it, though public trade results still let attentive players estimate it (R29). Initiative is no longer a single winner but an ordering of all players by bid + d2 (R27). Initiative is used for exactly two things. First, players pick their place in a turn order that holds for the whole turn, in initiative order, so the top player may choose to act last (R26). Second, the top player decides the trading order during Trading itself and may change it until each trade resolves (R25). Initiative order also replaces "earliest submitted" as the tie-break between equal offers (R24). Every ship may jump once per turn, as separate per-ship decisions made one after another, and after each jump the player chooses where the ship ends up: Port, Planet, or Space (R26, R30). Each system now has those three locations with simple statistics and no mechanics yet (R31). Retailer is an explicit first market-position tier, not a default (R28). Factories produce units per turn that are lost unless stored (R32). A player may place at most one offer per good per system (R33). Unit amounts are small: single digits are typical and 12–14 is large (Q06 note). Screen-level choices are recorded as D07 and D08.

On 2026-10-29 the creator dropped timers altogether (D09, superseding D04 and D06). The players are a group of friends who want a good time without policing each other; anyone who is slow can be nudged over Discord or in-game chat. No phase or step has a deadline: the game waits for every player, and each screen names whoever it is waiting for. A06 (one absent player stalls the game) still holds, and now applies from the first absence.

Later on 2026-10-29 the creator replaced single-winner trade resolution with a counter (R34–R38). The roll stands for trading before the players arrive. The winning offer trades first, then any offer the current counter still allows, the most units first. The price changes once at the end, while the S/D indicator stays fixed and is rolled against afresh each turn. Every good resolves every turn, so prices drift even without offers. Wholesalers trade up to 5 u at the new price, and the Dictator places their offer only at resolution, after seeing the disclosed offers and before the roll, without a trump; they can't bid for that good in Bidding.

Also on 2026-10-29: a game ends after turn 20, and the player with the most cash wins; assets don't count (R39).

The same day the creator settled everything a first playtest needs. The game's creator picks its length and starts it once players have joined (R41). Everyone gets 300 HT and 2 empty ships placed at random Ports in different systems (R40). Cargo is counted only for bulky goods (POLY, ISO), 10 per ship, and the player picks which ship loads or unloads (R42). Trading 6 or more units can move a player up a market-position tier on a 2d6 roll (R43). Warehouses and factories are out of v1 (N08). Screens use the design system, and the first playtest runs locally behind ngrok (D10).

## Scope

- **Now:** multi-game hosting with a game/room boundary; shared-state vs. per-player-confidential-state model; polling endpoint(s) for state sync (D01); persistent per-game state in a document store (D02); passwordless email-code login and session (D03); phase engine that gates ordered actions (e.g. ship jumps) until the simultaneous phase closes, waiting for every player with no timers (D09); a way for one player to transfer or sell confidential info to another (R06); trade resolution via the existing price/dice formula, granting a right to trade 1-to-max rather than a forced amount (R08, R13); a granular post-trade reference-price move, settling the actual trade at the winner's own price, with extreme bids saturating rather than being unbounded (R14, R15); a won trade updates the player's money and market-position tier, with a sell capped by ship/warehouse inventory (R10, R11) — blocked on Q03 (the inventory model itself) before this part can be built — and a buy capped only by affordability, with storage overflow simply lost (R12, R16); a v1 turn of exactly three phases — secret, simultaneous Bidding (R17, plus an all-pay money bid for initiative, R21), then an open, initiative-ordered Ship-jump phase where each player picks one ship's destination (R19/R20), then sequential public Trading using the resulting ship positions (R18, R23) — repeated, with the other 5 board-game phases out of scope entirely (N06); each player starting with a fixed money balance and 3 fixed-capacity ships, no purchasing/commissioning for v1 (R22, N07); a ship must be present at a system to trade there, with goods bought at a shipless system simply lost (R23) — this whole slice exists to playtest login, turns, trading, and visuals, not to be feature-complete.
- **Later:** push-based sync (WebSocket/SSE) if polling proves too slow or too costly at scale (N01); direct player-to-player chat, requested by the creator as "would be nice" but not committed for v1 (R07); reconnection/resilience hardening — decided but not yet built: a disconnected player can resume their seat at any time via re-login (D05); the game simply waits for them (D09); no removal/replace mechanism exists for v1 (N04); abuse/rate-limit handling for the email-code login.
- **Not doing:** see Non-goals.

## Key flows

- Current state: a player can submit a trade offer and the server can resolve the best offer for a system+good with a dice roll — but only within one global, unscoped list; there is no login, no game selection, and no persistence. `[PRODUCT]` `TradeOfferController.kt`.
- Future state (proposed, not yet built):
  1. Player signs in with name, star-corporation name and email; receives a one-time code by email; code exchange starts a session (D03).
  2. Player joins or creates a game (room); game state is created/loaded from the document store (D02).
  3. During simultaneous phases, each player's client polls the game's state endpoint every few seconds; the server advances the phase once all players have acted; there is no timer, and the game shows who it is waiting for (D01, D09).
  4. During ordered phases (e.g. ship jumps), the server resolves actions in a fixed sequence rather than waiting on a simultaneous deadline.
  5. A player may spend an action to share or sell a piece of confidential info with another named player, moving it from their own confidential state into the recipient's (R06).
  6. A v1 turn is exactly three phases — Bidding, Ship-jumps, then Trading — repeated (the original board game's other 5 turn phases don't exist for v1 at all, N06). In Bidding, a player may submit any number of secret trade offers across different systems/goods (R17), and place a money bid, 0 or more, to compete for that turn's initiative (R21) — both invisible to other players, including via any listing endpoint, until the relevant phase reveals them (the current `/api/trade-offers` endpoint does not yet enforce this for offers, a known gap tying into A05). Signaling done is a readiness flag, not a commit — a player may keep submitting offers until Bidding actually closes, once every player has signaled done (D09).
  7. Initiative resolves next: the player with the highest bid plus a d2 roll holds that turn's initiative, paying their own bid regardless of outcome — every bidder pays, not just the winner (R21; ties are rerolled until broken).
  8. In the Ship-jump phase, each player — one at a time, in the order the initiative holder sets — openly picks one of their ships and a destination system; the jump resolves instantly, no fuel, hull damage, repair, or multi-cycle transit for v1, and any of the 6 systems may be picked regardless of the ship's current location (R19, R20).
  9. (Rewritten 2026-10-29 by R34–R38; the original text follows for history.) In Trading, every system+good with pending offers resolves one at a time; the complete order was set up front by whichever player held that turn's initiative, unrelated to the offers themselves or to the existing per-offer submission-sequence tiebreak (R18). Each resolution works as follows: adding a 2d6 roll to the system's per-good indicator: ≥0 picks the best BUY offer (highest price, earliest submitted as tiebreak), negative picks the best SELL offer (lowest price, earliest submitted) (R08). Winning grants a right to trade between 1 and a maximum amount — the gap between the dice-resolved position and the indicator implied by the winning price, saturating at ±7/-6 worth of reference-price movement for an extreme bid rather than growing unbounded (R13, R15). The system's reference price then moves by exactly the amount actually executed, one step at a time; the trade itself always settles at the winner's own stated price regardless (R14). The winner's money and market-position tier then update. Selling requires a ship the player holds to actually be at that system — now settled, since Ship-jumps already resolved this turn — and is capped by that ship's (and warehouse's, pending Q03) holdings (R10, R23); a buy is capped only by what the player can currently afford, with the goods going into a present ship's cargo (overflow beyond capacity lost, R16) or lost entirely if the player has no ship at that system at all (R23). Every resolution is immediately visible to all players (R18).
  9a. Since 2026-10-29: every good at every system resolves in the first initiative player's order (R36). Offers are disclosed, the Dictator may place their offer, which they can only do here (R38), then the counter starts at S/D + 2d6. The winning offer trades first, then whichever offer allows the most units at the current counter, each offer once, all amounts from the start price (R34). The price then changes once by the counter's final position, clamped to 1–20 (R35), and Wholesalers without an offer, plus the Dictator, may trade up to 5 u at the new price (R37).
  10. (Scope/Later) If a player disconnects or closes the tab, they can simply log back in (step 1) and rejoin their same seat with all state intact, at any time (D05). The game waits for them (D09); there is no auto-skip, so the stall risk (A06) applies from the first absence, since no removal mechanism exists (N04).
